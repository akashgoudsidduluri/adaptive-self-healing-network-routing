DYNAMIC PATH SELECTION

Intelligent bypass around congested corridors.

Static routing algorithms like naive OSPF send all traffic across the lowest nominal hop count until links saturate. NetAdapt measures live link utilization and dynamically inflates costs on loaded links.

  • ✓
    Cost Function: Combines propagation delay, queue occupancy, and nominal bandwidth.
  • ✓
    Oscillation Prevention: Hysteresis smoothing prevents unstable packet flapping between routes.
  • ✓
    Per-Flow Coherence: Ensures TCP packets maintain packet order during path migrations.

TEST SCENARIO 01 · LINK FAILURE & RECOVERY

How to test Self-Healing in the Lab.

Follow these exact steps in the interactive workspace to witness real-time link failure detection, autonomous Dijkstra SPF recalculation, and traffic failover with zero packet loss.

1
Load Topology & Launch Traffic

In the top menu, choose Preset Self-Healing Demo. Click PC1, set Destination to PC2, Packets to 20, Rate to 5 pps, and click Start traffic.

2
Sever Active Cable Link (Inject Fault)

While packets are actively streaming across R1 → R2, click directly on the physical cable between R1 and R2. In the right Inspector panel, click the red Fail link button.

3
Observe Real-Time Autonomous Rerouting

The cable turns dashed red. The simulator detects the break, emits LINK_FAILED, and instantaneously shifts subsequent packets via R3.

4
Heal the Link & Observe Route Recovery

Click the severed link cable again and click Recover link. Adjacency heartbeats re-establish, and the flow automatically migrates back to the primary low-cost path.

VERIFICATION CHECKLIST
  • ✓ Timeline Event: Emits LINK_FAILED then ROUTE_RECALCULATED within 50ms.
  • ✓ Live Route: Changes dynamically from PC1→SW1→R1→R2→SW2→PC2 to ...R1→R3→SW2...
  • ✓ Packet Delivery Ratio: Maintains 100.0% PDR with zero packet loss after failover.

TEST SCENARIO 02 · QOS PREEMPTION & SCHEDULING

How to test QoS Preemption in the Lab.

Quality of Service ensures life-safety or latency-critical data is not delayed behind heavy bulk streams. Test preemption live:

1
Enable Priority Queue Policy

Click anywhere on the canvas background to open Lab Policies. Under Quality of Service, ensure the Scheduler dropdown is set to Priority Queue and click Apply QoS policy.

2
Send Background Bulk Video Traffic

Click PC1. Configure Destination = PC2, Class = Video, Packets = 30, Rate = 5 pps. Click Start traffic.

3
Inject Emergency Traffic While Video is Queued

Immediately while Video packets are draining, change Class to Emergency, Packets = 5, and click Start traffic.

4
Watch Priority Overtaking in Real-Time

Emergency packets instantly jump to the front of the queue, popping out onto the wire ahead of the waiting Video packets.

VERIFICATION CHECKLIST
  • ✓ Queue Preemption: Emergency packets are delivered first with ~20ms latency.
  • ✓ Video Queuing Delay: Remaining Video packets safely wait in queue with higher delay (~2000ms+) without drops.
  • ✓ WFQ Comparison: Switch to WFQ to observe proportional interleaved service (3:1 ratio).

TEST SCENARIO 03 · TOKEN BUCKET REGULATION

How to test Traffic Shaping in the Lab.

Token Bucket algorithm smooths traffic bursts and enforces service level agreements without dropping compliant packets.

1
Open Traffic Shaping Panel

Click on the background to open policies, then click Open Traffic Shaping panel. Set Status to ENABLED, Rate to 5 packets/s, and Capacity to 10.

2
Test Burst Within Capacity (Immediate Flow)

Click PC1, select Destination = Server1, Packets = 8, click Start traffic. All 8 packets transmit instantly without queuing delay because 8 ≤ 10 capacity.

3
Test Burst Exceeding Capacity (Rate Regulated)

Send a larger burst of 20 packets. Observe: the first 10 packets consume all available tokens and transmit immediately. The remaining 10 packets buffer in the shaper queue.

4
Observe Paced Release & Shaping Delay

As tokens accumulate at 5/sec, packets release into the scheduler in exact steady cadence. In the packet viewer, each delayed packet displays its measured Shaping delay.

VERIFICATION CHECKLIST
  • ✓ Queue Counter: Displays active shaping queue count: (N shaping).
  • ✓ Shaping Delay: Measured in milliseconds on packets exceeding token capacity.
  • ✓ Zero Loss: Unlike hard policing, shaping queues rather than drops excess packets.

TEST SCENARIO 04 · MAC & ARP DISCOVERY

How to inspect MAC & ARP in the Lab.

Inspect how Ethernet switches construct local MAC tables and how hosts query ARP tables before sending IP packets.

1
Inspect Switch MAC Table Before Traffic

Click on SW1. Scroll down the Inspector to MAC address table. Notice it initially reports no learned entries for hosts that haven't transmitted.

2
Transmit Packet & Observe Learning

Click PC1 and send 1 packet to PC2. As the frame passes through SW1, SW1 inspects the source MAC and binds it to port eth0.

3
Highlight Port on Canvas

Click the newly learned MAC entry in the Inspector table. The physical link connecting PC1 to SW1 will highlight in green on the canvas.

4
Run ICMP Echo Diagnostic (Ping)

Select PC1, set Protocol to ICMP Echo, Destination = PC2, and click Start traffic. Observe real ARP cache resolution and measured round-trip time.

VERIFICATION CHECKLIST
  • ✓ Dynamic Learning: Switch learns source MACs automatically upon frame arrival.
  • ✓ Unicast Forwarding: Subsequent traffic to learned MACs is forwarded directly without flooding.
  • ✓ ARP Cache: Can be inspected and cleared via clear_arp or console command arp -d.
